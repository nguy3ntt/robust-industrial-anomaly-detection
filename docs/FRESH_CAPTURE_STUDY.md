# Fresh-capture pilot: collect first, claim results only after evaluation

Status: **workflow prepared; no fresh photographs supplied and no fresh-capture
accuracy results exist**. Synthetic shifts and test fixtures are not a real
capture study. Everything below runs locally; no accounts or new dataset
downloads are needed. Do not upload your photographs or commit the study folder.

## Choose the right question

The implemented pilot tests the **unchanged deployed product model** under
fresh physical capture sessions. It does not train a new category, recalibrate
thresholds, select a new gate, or prove factory readiness. Use a supported
product with a meaningful match to its existing VisA normal reference. The
default `candle` is a template, not proof that any candle arrangement matches
the reference. Explain the physical product, arrangement, framing and mismatch
in `protocol.json` before freezing.

If you want to photograph unrelated objects such as washers or new electronic
parts, stop before freezing: they need their own normal training/calibration
references and a separately registered adaptation study. Do not relabel them
as an existing product just to get a score. Once you choose the product and
have development photos, we can review whether this transfer question is valid.

## 1. Initialize an empty local study

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/capture_study.py init --category candle
```

Default folder: `artifacts/capture-studies/fresh-capture-pilot/` (ignored by
Git). Already initialized on this workstation; do not reinitialize it. For a
larger collection use `--study D:\your-private-capture-study` consistently,
before collecting/freezing. `init` never overwrites an existing folder.

The folder contains `protocol.json`, `sessions.csv`, an empty `captures.csv`,
and empty `development/` and `evaluation/` directories. No images, labels,
freezes or fabricated experimental results are generated by initialization.

## 2. Make a physical collection plan

- Photograph only objects/images you own or are authorized to use. Exclude
  people, address labels, screens and sensitive production information.
- Keep original, static JPEG/PNG photographs at most 20 MiB and 40 MP. No image
  editor, synthetic blur/noise, screenshots, web/dataset images, burst copies
  or AI-generated pictures. HEIC must be exported consistently before use;
  record that preprocessing and do not selectively enhance individual images.
- Use two genuinely different cameras (a borrowed phone is fine) and two real
  lighting arrangements. Do not rename one camera to simulate two. If this is
  unavailable, keep collecting development photos; the planned two-camera
  evaluation cannot honestly be marked complete.
- Use separate capture sessions, resetting position/lighting/background between
  sessions. A session is a physical acquisition period, not an arbitrary CSV ID.
- Keep physical objects and sessions disjoint between development and final
  evaluation. All views and before/after modifications of one object retain
  the SAME `object_id`, even if its label changes.
- Define visible defects in advance. Safe physical surrogate marks, if used,
  must be identified as staged marks, not manufacturing defects. Never damage
  powered devices, batteries, safety equipment or somebody else's property.

Edit `sessions.csv` to describe the actual planned setup. The template provides
two development sessions and four future evaluation sessions crossing camera A/B
with diffuse/side lighting. Keep both normal and anomalous objects in **every**
evaluation session, camera and lighting group so labels are not confounded with
capture conditions. Freeze this plan before final collection.

Collect a few development examples of both labels to check framing and label
definitions. Do not treat them as training data or change this model's thresholds
from their outcomes. If development motivates a model change, finish that work
and register a separate appropriate protocol before acquiring the final set.

Complete `protocol.json`: actual product description, domain-match reasoning,
image rights and fresh-photograph confirmations. Leave confirmations false
until they are true. Owner attestations and timestamps support an audit trail;
software cannot independently prove that a photograph is new or a label correct.

## 3. Register every photograph

Put development files in `development/`. Add one row per original capture to
`captures.csv` (keep the header exactly as generated):

```text
sample_id,image_path,object_id,session_id,label,captured_at,defect_note
dev-001,development/photo-001.jpg,object-001,dev-a,normal,2026-10-18T10:00:00+10:00,
dev-002,development/photo-002.jpg,object-002,dev-a,anomaly,2026-10-18T10:01:00+10:00,Describe the visible physical defect
```

These are illustrative metadata rows, **not** supplied images. Replace dates,
paths, descriptions and IDs with your actual acquisitions. Anomalies require
a defect note. Timestamp includes timezone; session ID links to `sessions.csv`.
IDs use letters/digits/hyphens/underscores, at most 80 characters; avoid Windows
reserved names and case-only sample-ID differences. Retain every
registered capture, including poor captures and false alerts; do not curate
the final set based on model outcomes.

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/capture_study.py audit
```

The auditor checks rights/metadata, safe paths, complete file registration,
image decoding, hashes, exact/decoded duplicates, cross-partition perceptual
duplicates on full-frame and model-crop views, and object/session separation.
Near-duplicate checks are conservative heuristics, not proof of independence.
Audit decodes pixels only for integrity; it does not infer, render panels or
report accuracy. Dataset membership/freshness still requires honest collection.

## 4. Freeze before collecting any final photographs

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/capture_study.py freeze
```

Requires real audited development examples and a verified local M10 inspector.
It locks the model, gate, localization, normal memories, calibration, source
code, dependency lock, protocol/session plan and development membership to
SHA-256 identities. `evaluation/` must still be empty and there must be no final
CSV rows. No fitting or threshold search occurs. Save the printed commitment
hash independently if you want an additional record of preregistration.

Only after `freeze.json` exists, acquire final photos in the planned sessions
and register them under `evaluation/`. The minimum pilot is 20 normal and 20
anomalous captures across at least 20 distinct final objects, four sessions,
two cameras and two lighting conditions. More independent objects/sessions are
better than many repeated views. These modest counts are a practical pilot,
not a power calculation or a guarantee of a precise false-alarm estimate.

Final timestamps must follow the freeze. Do not inspect final photos in the
website or any other model, tune thresholds, change masks/labels from predictions,
or remove failures before the first fixed evaluation. Integrity audit sees
pixels, but model output stays withheld. Keep source quality problems in the
study so recapture burden is measurable.

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/capture_study.py audit --evaluation
```

Changing code/model artifacts/protocol/session plan/development membership after
freeze fails closed. Do not delete the commitment or reuse an exposed final set
to call an altered model “untouched”; use a new registered study and new final
objects/sessions. Hashes are accidental-change checks, not access control or
independent signed proof against someone rewriting the whole local study.

## 5. One fixed evaluation, with safe exact resume

Once the real photographs and frozen evaluation audit are ready:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/capture_study.py evaluate --unlock-holdout
```

This records `holdout-opened.json` **before** first inference. The unchanged
model runs once on every final photo, including poor quality captures.
Per-image metadata checkpoints allow the exact command to resume after an
interruption; different images, metadata, code, device or runtime artifacts
are refused. A repeat with identical inputs is a reproduction/resume, not a new
independent evaluation. No new server or network upload is involved.

`summary.json` and `group_metrics.csv` report false alerts, recapture/review,
confident defect recall, missed defects, operator action, automatic coverage,
sample/object/session counts and local inspection timing. Conditional
evaluated-only false alerts/recall are secondary and undefined if none pass the
gate; never use low all-capture FPR alone to call an always-rejecting gate good.
Every final capture
stays in the primary denominators; recapture is unresolved, never successful
defect detection. Results are stratified by session, camera and lighting.
No image-independent confidence intervals are claimed for repeated objects or
sessions. Local timing includes normal inference/rendering stages, but not
acquisition, browser transport or total operator work.

This first pilot has image labels, not pixel masks: it does **not** establish
localization accuracy, spatial stability, calibrated fresh-domain guarantees,
or physical defect causality. Predictions, source metadata and outputs stay in
the private study folder. Public aggregate publication needs a later privacy/
licence/interpretation review; there is no automatic Git publication.
